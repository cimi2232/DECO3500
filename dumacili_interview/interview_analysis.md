# Interview 1

## Summary

The participant described the pre-concert experience as a sequence of **queueing, ticket checking, finding the correct gate, buying drinks, waiting, and interacting with friends**.

Their experience suggests that venue communication and crowd management can strongly affect the emotional experience before a concert. Shared interest in the artist can encourage spontaneous interaction, while long queues, crowding, line-cutting, and poor communication can reduce excitement.

---

## Q1 — Arrival and Pre-show Routine

### Transcript

> "First, you wait in the queue to get into the concert and then they scan the QR code or the ticket and then they let you in."

**Code:** `Queueing as part of entry process`  
**Category:** Entry & Waiting

> "There are different gates. You have to go to whichever gate you belong to."

**Code:** `Navigating to assigned entrance`  
**Category:** Wayfinding & Navigation

> "I'll just go grab some drinks."

**Code:** `Pre-show refreshment activity`  
**Category:** Pre-show Activity

> "I just wait until the performance starts, or talk to my friends or hype each other up about the songs... the artist or the event."

**Code:** `Building excitement with friends`  
**Category:** Social Interaction & Anticipation

### Insight

Waiting before the concert is not necessarily inactive time. Audience members already use this period to **socialise and build excitement with friends**.

---

## Q2 — Separation and Wayfinding

### Transcript

> "I got lost because I got confused with the other gate."

**Code:** `Gate confusion`  
**Category:** Wayfinding & Navigation

> "My friend group was on the other side and I was on the other side of the venue."

**Code:** `Group separation caused by wayfinding confusion`  
**Category:** Group Coordination

### Insight

Confusing venue navigation can create more than an individual wayfinding problem. It can also cause **group separation and coordination difficulties**.

## Q3 — Safety and Crowded Environments

### Transcript

**Code:** `Group presence affects perceived safety`  
**Category:** Crowd Experience & Safety

### Insight

Perceived safety in crowded environments may depend on the **presence and behaviour of surrounding groups**, rather than crowd density alone.

# Interview 2

## Summary

The participant described a relatively calm pre-concert experience involving **ticket check-in, buying drinks, finding a good spot, and waiting**.

Unlike Interview 1, ordinary queueing was not viewed negatively and could contribute to anticipation. However, the participant described a significant coordination problem when crowding caused **mobile reception to fail and a friend became separated from the group**.

---

## Q1 — Arrival and Pre-show Routine

### Transcript

> "We checked in with the tickets, and then we bought ourselves some drinks."

**Code:** `Straightforward entry process`  
**Category:** Entry & Pre-show Activity

> "I tried to find a good spot because it was open seating."

**Code:** `Finding a preferred viewing position`  
**Category:** Spatial Navigation

### Insight

Finding and securing a suitable position can be an important part of **pre-show audience behaviour**.

---

## Q2 — Separation and Mobile Communication

### Transcript

> "It was so crowded."

**Code:** `High crowd density`  
**Category:** Crowd Experience

> "Even the cell phones weren't working because when there are lots of people, there's no reception."

**Code:** `Loss of mobile connectivity in crowds`  
**Category:** Mobile Communication

> "We lost one friend, and we technically couldn't find him at all."

**Code:** `Unable to locate separated group member`  
**Category:** Group Coordination

> "We got separated in the afternoon, and it wasn't until the evening that this guy just showed up."

**Code:** `Prolonged separation`  
**Category:** Group Coordination

### Insight

Crowded environments create an important **social-mobile tension**:

> Attendees rely on mobile phones to coordinate with friends, but mobile connectivity may become unreliable exactly when coordination is most necessary.

---

## Q3 — Safety

### Transcript

> "I don't recall being unsafe or uneasy."

**Code:** `No strong perception of physical danger`  
**Category:** Safety

> "I may have felt it at times if I was just by myself."

**Code:** `Being alone may affect comfort`  
**Category:** Safety & Social Support

### Insight

Comfort in crowded environments may depend partly on whether attendees are **alone or accompanied by familiar people**.

---

## Q4 — Shared Music and Social Connection

### Transcript

> "We were listening to this guy's music because he was about to play some new things."

**Code:** `Pre-show music engagement`  
**Category:** Pre-show Engagement

> "We sort of did our homework and listened to all of his music."

**Code:** `Preparing for shared concert experience`  
**Category:** Shared Experience

> "We were exploring the music and having some good conversations around it."

**Code:** `Music facilitates pre-show conversation`  
**Category:** Social Interaction

### Insight

Music-related activities can extend the concert experience into the waiting period and create opportunities for **conversation and social bonding**.

---

## Q5 — Venue Information and Wayfinding

### Transcript

> "They put up road signs and manage the traffic."

**Code:** `Physical signage supports navigation`  
**Category:** Wayfinding & Crowd Management

> "They also put information... in one place with proper signage."

**Code:** `Centralised event information`  
**Category:** Venue Communication

> "They're planning to have a separate app so that people have access to information about the event."

**Code:** `Mobile access to event information`  
**Category:** Mobile Information

> **Evidence Note:** The participant did not directly experience the proposed app. Therefore, this should be treated as lower-strength evidence rather than a direct user experience.

### Insight

Clear and centralised information can support **navigation and crowd movement**, while mobile information may provide an additional communication channel.

---

## Q6 — Queue Experience

### Transcript

> "I just have patience. You just wait for your turn."

**Code:** `Acceptance of waiting`  
**Category:** Waiting Experience

> "You cannot do anything about it."

**Code:** `Waiting perceived as unavoidable`  
**Category:** Waiting Experience

> "You see other people getting into the event and then you're excited."

**Code:** `Queue builds anticipation`  
**Category:** Waiting & Emotion

> "If it's a very, very big queue, I can imagine you would just feel exhausted."

**Code:** `Long queues may lead to exhaustion`  
**Category:** Waiting & Emotion

### Insight

Waiting is **not automatically negative**. A manageable queue can contribute to anticipation, while excessive waiting may transform excitement into exhaustion.
