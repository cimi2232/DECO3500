# 2. Thematic Analysis

The interview responses were coded and grouped into broader themes based on repeated behaviours, concerns, and social needs.

| Initial Codes | Theme |
|---|---|
| Eye contact, smiling, facial expressions, clear social cues, uncertainty about approaching | **Social Readiness & Non-verbal Signals** |
| Introversion, social boundaries, control over timing, avoiding unwanted interaction | **User Control & Social Boundaries** |
| Shared fandom, similar music taste, common topics, singing together | **Shared Interests as Social Connection** |
| Long waiting time, boredom, queueing, pre-show activities | **Pre-concert Waiting as a Social Opportunity** |
| Lightweight device, physical comfort, weather, privacy, age and gender preferences | **Comfort, Privacy & Personalisation** |

---

# 3. Key Themes and UX Insights

## Theme 1: Social Readiness & Non-verbal Signals

### Evidence

> "I usually look at eye contact and facial expressions."

> "I think some clear non-verbal cues would help."

### Finding

The participant currently relies on subtle social cues such as eye contact, smiling, and facial expressions to judge whether another person is willing to interact.

### UX Insight

**A wearable system could provide a clearer but still subtle signal showing whether a solo concert-goer is currently open to social interaction.**

---

## Theme 2: User Control & Social Boundaries

### Evidence

> "I'd prefer them to approach me when I'm free and actually open to socialising."

### Finding

The participant is willing to socialise but wants control over when other people approach her.

### UX Insight

**Users should be able to easily change their social availability, helping reduce unwanted approaches while still supporting spontaneous interaction.**

---

## Theme 3: Shared Interests as Social Connection

### Evidence

> "We're fans of the same musician or artist, or we have similar tastes."

### Finding

Shared artists, music tastes, and fandom experiences naturally create common topics between strangers.

### UX Insight

**Shared interests could be used as lightweight conversation starters to help solo concert-goers interact more naturally.**

---

## Theme 4: Pre-concert Waiting as a Social Opportunity

### Evidence

> "The waiting time can be quite boring."

> "Even after entering the venue, there can still be a long wait before the performance starts."

### Finding

Long queues and waiting periods before concerts create extended periods of inactivity and boredom.

### UX Insight

**Pre-concert waiting periods could be transformed into opportunities for lightweight social interaction or shared activities.**

---

## Theme 5: Comfort, Privacy & Personalisation

### Evidence

> "I wouldn't like it if the device was too heavy or too enclosed."

> "I think being able to filter by gender and age would be useful."

### Finding

The participant values both physical comfort and control over personal information when using a wearable social device.

### UX Insight

**The wearable should be lightweight, comfortable in different weather conditions, and allow users to control what personal information is visible to others.**

---

# 4. Key Design Opportunities

Based on the thematic analysis, several design opportunities emerged:

1. **Communicate social availability**
   - Allow users to clearly show whether they are open to interaction.

2. **Reduce uncertainty before approaching**
   - Provide subtle cues that help users understand whether someone wants to talk.

3. **Give users control over social boundaries**
   - Allow users to change their availability depending on their mood or situation.

4. **Use shared music interests as conversation starters**
   - Support interaction through shared artists, songs, or fandom interests.

5. **Use waiting time for social interaction**
   - Introduce lightweight activities or social games during queues and pre-show waiting periods.

6. **Support privacy and selective information sharing**
   - Allow users to decide what information they want to display and who can access it.

7. **Prioritise wearable comfort**
   - Keep the wearable lightweight, non-restrictive, and suitable for concert environments.

---

# 5. Core Insight

The participant is not unwilling to socialise. Instead, the main challenge is uncertainty around whether other people are willing to interact and whether it is an appropriate time to approach them.

> **Solo concert-goers may be open to social interaction but need clearer signals of social willingness and greater control over when interactions occur.**

This suggests an opportunity to design a lightweight wearable system that communicates social availability without requiring users to directly initiate a conversation.
